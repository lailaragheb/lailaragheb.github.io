Evolving a Wallet's Content for an Investment Product

Context
Bilingual UX content case study, completed as a take-home exercise for a Senior UX Writer role at a wallet app
Role
UX Writer
Scope
Content for three investment products (saving goals, gold, and stocks), plus three rewrites of difficult account messages, in English and moderate Egyptian Arabic
Team
Individual exercise
Tools
Figma Slides
Status
Completed as a take-home exercise
ProblemA wallet app was adding a wealth section for first-time savers, with no financial voice guide and unconfirmed rates and fees.
What I didI wrote my own content principles, then the English and Arabic content for saving goals, gold, and stocks, and rewrote three difficult account messages.
ResultA complete bilingual content set with risk warnings and costs shown at the moment of decision.

The brief

Write the content for a wallet app's new wealth section, for people who are new to saving or investing and have mixed levels of financial literacy, in English and Arabic.

There was no brand voice guide for financial products, and exact rates, fees, and minimums weren't confirmed. So I wrote my own content principles first and used placeholder figures.

The content principles

  • Accessible: plain language over financial jargon, short active sentences, and an 8th-grade reading level at most.
  • Trustworthy: transparent and reassuring, with no over-promising. Always explain why something is blocked or pending.
  • Human: sound like a knowledgeable person, not a chatbot.
  • Action-oriented: every screen makes the next step obvious. "Start Saving" and "Buy Gold" instead of "Submit" or "Continue".

Decisions that shaped the work

  • Naming it a "saving goal". It isn't technically a savings account, so I didn't call it one.
  • A conversational Arabic voice. I used moderate Egyptian Arabic so the experience feels friendly, not institutional.
  • Risk warnings at the decision moment. For gold and stocks, the warning sits right above the confirm button, where the user is about to commit.
  • Costs in plain sight. Commission, total cost, and storage fee appear as separate lines. Helper links such as "How is interest calculated?" and "How does digital gold work?" explain the unfamiliar parts without cluttering the screen.
  • Lowering the barrier. For stocks, "No need to buy a whole share" and the low starting amount address the most common fear of new investors. The button itself shows the total, as in "Buy with EGP 50.50".

Three hard messages, rewritten

Branch visit for KYC. The original said digital onboarding is complete, but the user must visit a branch to sign KYC documents. My version: "We're almost done with the account setup! The last step is a quick in-person ID check at any branch which usually takes about 10 minutes." It tells the user that only one step is left, and the time estimate counters the "bank queue" stereotype.

Suspicious activity. The original said the wallet is deactivated due to suspicious behavior and can be reactivated by updating KYC. My version: "Your wallet is temporarily on hold for security reasons, but your money is still safe. Update your personal information to reactivate it." It says the hold is reversible, that the balance is safe, and what to do next.

Permanent deactivation. The original said the wallet is permanently deactivated due to regulatory abuse. My version: "Your wallet has been permanently deactivated due to activity that doesn't comply with regulatory requirements. Contact support for more information." It makes clear that the account's activity caused the deactivation, and it still points to help.
