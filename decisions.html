Story 01 · Working within compliance

A fraud warning that still sounds like the bank

The constraint

Legal and compliance wrote the wording for the card-purchase OTP SMS and said the regulator would not accept anything looser. It read like a regulation and sounded nothing like the app's Arabic voice.

What I did

A customer used to one tone in the app could suddenly get a stiff message in another, in a message whose job is to warn about fraud. That could itself look like phishing. My first rewrite was rejected: they said banks do not word this message that way and the central bank would not accept it. So I stopped arguing tone against compliance and asked which words could not change. They named two phrases. I kept both and rewrote everything around them in the app's voice.

What changed

The approved SMS keeps the required wording, sounds like the bank, and now also says the day and time of the purchase.

Story 02 · Pushing back on a design

The screen I refused to write for

The situation

A new requirement from the product owner arrived late. Its screen was designed before any content existed, and I was brought in at the last minute to polish the copy.

What I did

The screen asked someone requesting a supplementary card for a family member to make about four decisions at once, and to compare three unrelated options. No copy could fix that. It needed two or three screens, so I declined to write for it. After a lot of debate we found the requirements themselves were wrong: a user can only request a card for a direct family member, and the cardholder does not have to have a bank account.

What changed

The requirements changed and the screen was discarded. The final flow splits the job into small steps, each with one decision.
