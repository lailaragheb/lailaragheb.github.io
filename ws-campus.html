Manual monitoring systems: How they threaten campus safety and how AI can help.

Writing sample

In recent years, campus crimes have become a growing concern for educational institutions worldwide as they pose a significant threat to the safety and security of school and university campuses. We have heard and read about several incidents that have happened in the past couple of years, one of which involved Iman Irsheed, a Jordanian nursing student, who was walking out of one of her exams at the Private University of Applied Sciences in Amman, when she was brutally sprayed with 5 consecutive bullets, one to the head and four to the body. It was later revealed that the shooter managed to enter the university campus disguised as a student. Security said he was able to get past the campus guards while carrying a weapon, then walked straight toward the victim, stopped in front of her, and shot her five times in broad daylight. The motive behind the crime hasn’t been revealed, and the Public Security Directorate confirmed that the killer shot himself after being surrounded by security forces at his home.

How poor campus security can put students’ lives in danger.

Unfortunately, this incident occurred just one day after a similar one, in which Nayera Ashraf, an Egyptian student from Mansoura, was slaughtered by her colleague right in front of the main gates of Mansoura University. While we might not know what caused such crimes and the details behind them, we can safely assume that poor campus security is the reason these incidents, and several similar ones, were not prevented.

Murders aren’t the only bone-chilling campus crimes we’ve heard and read about. Other crimes on campus, like theft, assault, or vandalism, can also have severe consequences for students, faculty, and staff. According to U.S. statistics for 2019, there were a staggering 27,300 criminal incidents reported to police and security agencies on college campuses. Relative to the number of students, that means there were approximately 18.7 on-campus crimes reported for every 10,000 full-time-equivalent (FTE) students.

These crimes not only jeopardize the safety and well-being of students, faculty, and staff, but also affect the overall campus environment and reputation. The importance of addressing and preventing campus crimes cannot be overstated, and relying solely on manual monitoring methods has its limitations and can contribute to the dangers of such crimes.

The currently used manual monitoring process and its limitations.

Manual campus monitoring is the process where human inspectors are responsible for reviewing extensive hours of surveillance recordings to identify any unusual occurrences or deviations happening on campus. These surveillance inspectors rely heavily on cognitive abilities to observe abnormal behavior and document it by hand. If any incident that needs urgent action is detected by the inspectors, they communicate it as fast as possible to security personnel physically patrolling the campus to resolve such incidents.

Currently, most universities and schools rely only on manual campus monitoring to maintain the safety and security of the campus environment. However, based on the statistics and incidents mentioned earlier, it is time to realize that the manual monitoring method has its limitations and is not providing the required safety for students and staff on campus.

One limitation of manual monitoring is delayed response. Human inspectors must spend hours watching surveillance recordings, which is time-consuming and prone to human error caused by fatigue and distraction, and can lead to delayed responses or missed incidents. In addition, manual monitoring often relies on reviewing hours of recorded surveillance footage. This process is labor-intensive and leaves room for crucial incidents to slip through the cracks. Security personnel may struggle to analyze vast amounts of data effectively, potentially missing critical details or patterns that could help prevent or solve crimes. Tools that analyze large volumes of data efficiently and accurately can help identify potential threats faster and provide valuable insights for proactive security measures.

Inconsistency and subjectivity are also limitations of the manual process, since human inspectors in a manual monitoring system can be susceptible to personal bias, subjective judgment, and inconsistency in identifying and responding to security threats. Subjectivity can compromise the reliability and standardization of security protocols, leading to potential gaps in campus security. This is why an objective and consistent analysis of this amount of data is needed to reach a higher level of reliability.

As campuses grow and security needs evolve, manual monitoring systems can struggle to scale up and adapt effectively. Leading education thinkers at the Universities and Colleges Admissions Service (UCAS) anticipate a 30% increase in demand for higher education. This growth in demand means campuses will need to expand, and new educational institutions will need to open, to accept more students worldwide. Hiring and training more staff can be costly and may not adequately address the increasing demands of campus security. This raises the need for an easily scalable, robust, and seamless solution for campus security that can adapt to the needs of different campus sizes and surveillance systems.

Addressing the manual campus monitoring limitations with AI.

With the dangers and limitations of manual campus monitoring systems becoming increasingly apparent, it’s clear that embracing advanced solutions like AI is crucial. By leveraging AI-driven technology, we can ensure a safer and more secure campus environment for students and staff.

We believe it’s time to prioritize the safety of our educational communities and take proactive steps towards a more secure future. So, we created INITOR, an advanced AI-driven platform specifically designed to address the limitations mentioned above and provide comprehensive solutions for campus monitoring. INITOR was developed with a wide range of features and benefits with one goal in mind: creating a safer space for our communities.

INITOR is an AI-powered facility monitoring system that provides surveillance cameras (CCTV) with intelligent monitoring capabilities. It can detect and identify persons, objects, and critical events, such as emergencies or fights, anywhere in the facility in real time.

Proactive prevention and rapid response are of utmost importance when it comes to campus safety. However, the problem with manual campus monitoring systems is that there will always be room for human error and delayed responses due to distractions, fatigue, or other factors. AI can be used to mitigate these risks by using real-time detection capabilities to enable security teams to take immediate action when potential threats are identified, which is exactly what we had in mind while implementing the instant detection feature in INITOR.

With INITOR’s smart platform, instant alerts are sent to the surveillance room personnel whenever an abnormality is detected, and the personnel can then decide whether it is dangerous enough to be passed on to security guards. This way, we can ensure a proactive approach to campus security, reducing the risks of crimes and providing a higher level of safety for students and staff.

What about the cost of integrating such a solution on large campuses? INITOR was made with scalability and adaptability in mind to meet the evolving security needs of educational institutions. Whether it’s a small school or a large university, INITOR can seamlessly integrate with existing CCTV systems, eliminating the need for costly infrastructure upgrades.

This flexibility allows campuses to enhance their security measures without disrupting day-to-day operations and without spending thousands on installation. Also, with the normal manual process, a large number of inspectors are needed to monitor all surveilled areas on campus. However, using INITOR can be cost-effective since only one or two human inspectors will be needed in the surveillance room, since they will be alerted automatically to abnormalities and won’t need to monitor constantly.
