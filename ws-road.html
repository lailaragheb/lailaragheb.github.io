Road safety hazards: What causes them and how to minimize them

Writing sample

It was a normal summer day in July 2021, when a tragic incident occurred in a village in Egypt’s Gharbia governorate, causing the death of a 36-year-old man. On the day of the incident, three men fell into an open sewer pit. Trying to save them, Amr El Sawi, the owner of a shop near the open pit, rushed into it to help the three men, not knowing that his courageous act might kill him. He was so focused on helping them that he did not notice how deep the pit was or how poorly ventilated it would be, and he suffocated inside.

How poor city inspection can cost us lives.

Contrary to what you might think, Amr El Sawi did not die because of a normal accident that could happen to anyone, nor did he die because of his courageous act. It was later reported that government negligence was to blame, since the sewage project had been under construction for more than four years, and a sewer pit was still left open on a frequently traveled road with no regard for road safety. Open sewers aren’t the only road damage issues that cause accidents. Another well-known accident in 2017 involved a huge tree that had fallen across Central Avenue and was not reported quickly enough by the city inspection department. It led to a car accident that was reportedly caused by the driver not seeing the tree while driving at night.

City inspection is a process the local government performs regularly to monitor the road quality and street safety within a city. Currently, most city inspection worldwide is done by an employee who drives around the city, visually monitors the roads to track repair needs, and reports any safety issue to the relevant department so repairs can begin. While this process might help prevent some road accidents, it is often not enough, as it is time-consuming, expensive, and extremely dangerous. Manual inspection can mean months, even years, before road damage is repaired, which can lead to accidents like the ones mentioned earlier. Such accidents can be avoided with the right tools to automate and streamline city inspection.

Using AI to enhance city inspection: is it enough?

With rapid urbanization over the past few centuries, more than half of the world’s population now lives in urban areas. As a result, road networks in cities have grown significantly. For example, the total length of roads in the emirate of Dubai in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) alone has reached 18.5 thousand kilometers as of the year 2021. As more roads are built, cities face a bigger challenge in maintaining and inspecting them for safety and quality.

Although AI-based city inspection is already on the market, the approach most solutions take has limitations worth considering. Most follow the same approach: installing an AI-powered tool on every road to constantly monitor the city. This approach can work well technically, but considering cost and time, it would require years to finish thousands of installations across a city and maybe even millions of dollars in initial installation costs. In the long run, all these tools will need constant maintenance. Another limitation is the manpower needed to sort through all the damage detections the tools make, which would raise costs in the long run as well, making the technology a liability rather than a way to streamline inspection.
